# KratOS Software Solutions — Website Design Specification

**Status:** Locked  
**Primary direction:** Local Modern Utility + Operational Clarity  
**Brand personality:** Friendly + Capable  
**Primary audience:** Small-scale Sri Lankan business owners looking to digitize operations  
**Primary service positioning:** Software solutions company, with custom ERP/business digitization as the flagship offering

---

## 1. Brand Positioning

KratOS Software Solutions should present itself as a young but disciplined software company that understands local businesses and can build reliable systems around the way they already operate.

The website must communicate two ideas at the same time:

- **Friendly:** easy to approach, easy to understand, non-technical language, practical guidance.
- **Capable:** real projects, structured process, clear delivery, reliable support, polished presentation.

The intended client reaction is:

> “These people understand how my business works, and they look capable enough to build what I need.”

---

## 2. Audience

### Primary Audience

Small-scale local Sri Lankan business owners, especially those who currently rely on:

- Manual records
- Paper-based stock tracking
- Spreadsheets
- Separate billing/accounting tools
- Informal operational workflows
- Repetitive manual reporting

Current client examples include:

- Restaurant
- Poultry wholesale/retail business

The site should remain broad enough to speak to:

- Retail businesses
- Restaurants
- Wholesalers
- Small manufacturers
- Service businesses
- Other SMEs that need custom digital systems

---

## 3. Core Offer

KratOS does **not** sell a fixed one-size-fits-all ERP.

The company customizes systems according to:

- Business scale
- Workflow
- Required modules
- Existing processes
- Growth needs
- Budget

Some clients may need a full ERP, while others may only need selected functions such as:

- Sales
- Inventory
- Billing
- Purchasing
- Customer management
- Supplier management
- HR
- Payroll
- Reporting
- Accounting-related workflows

### Core Message

> **Your business, digitized your way.**

Supporting idea:

> Start with what you need today. Add more as your business grows.

---

## 4. Main Client Concerns

The site must actively reduce three major objections:

### 4.1 Cost

Clients may assume ERP solutions are too expensive.

**Response:**  
Communicate that pricing is based on the client's scale and actual requirements.

Preferred message:

> You only pay for what your business actually needs.

Do **not** show rigid public pricing packages at this stage.

### 4.2 Complexity

Clients may feel ERP systems are too complicated.

**Response:**  
Explain features using normal business language, not technical terminology.

Avoid language such as:

- Digital transformation ecosystem
- Enterprise resource orchestration
- Modular architecture
- Operational intelligence layer

Prefer:

- Track your stock
- Record sales
- Manage suppliers
- View reports
- Reduce manual work
- Keep everything in one place

### 4.3 Reliability

Because KratOS is a young team, some clients may question whether the company can deliver.

**Response:**  
Use proof instead of claims:

- Real client names
- Client logos
- Real ERP screenshots
- Project descriptions
- Clear process
- Ongoing support
- Professional visual presentation

---

## 5. Conversion Goals

### Primary CTA

**Message us on WhatsApp**

This should be the fastest and most visible conversion path.

Recommended placements:

- Header
- Hero
- Mid-page trust section
- Final CTA
- Mobile sticky action

### Secondary CTA

**Request a Quotation**

This should be available for users who already understand what they need.

The quotation form should remain simple and non-technical.

### Preferred Process

2. Initial discussion
3. Meeting / site visit
4. Workflow and requirements gathering
5. Scope definition
6. Quotation
7. Development
8. Testing
9. Deployment
10. Ongoing support

The public-facing version should be simplified to:

1. **Message us**
2. **We understand your business**
3. **We visit / discuss your workflow**
4. **We define what you need**
5. **We send a quotation**
6. **We build, test and support**

This process should feel reassuring, not technical.

## 7. Local Brand Approach

The website should feel clearly Sri Lankan and locally relatable.

### Use

- Real local business contexts
- Restaurant counters
- Retail shelves
- Stockrooms
- Supplier deliveries
- POS environments
- Invoices
- Cashier workflows
- Real project screenshots
- WhatsApp-first contact patterns

### Avoid

- Generic Western office stock images
- Corporate skyscraper imagery
- Over-polished fake enterprise dashboards
- Silicon Valley startup clichés
- Abstract “AI” visuals

The goal is:

> **Local understanding with professional software quality.**

## 8. Language Strategy

The website will be **fully trilingual**:

- English
- Sinhala
- Tamil

Language selector:

**EN | සිං | தமிழ்**

All three languages must be treated as first-class experiences.

Translate:

- Navigation
- Headings
- Body copy
- Forms
- Validation messages
- CTAs
- Case studies
- FAQs
- Contact information
- Process steps
- Footer content

Translation quality is part of the brand trust signal.

A partially translated or machine-translated experience is not acceptable.

The site should combine a strong long-form homepage with dedicated supporting pages.

### Recommended Main Navigation

- Home

- How We Work
- About
- Contact

### Page Structure

## 10. Locked Visual Direction

### Style Formula

**70% Local Modern Utility**  
**30% Operational Clarity**

Supporting tone:

- Friendly, not playful
- Modern, not trendy
- Technical, not intimidating
- Local, not amateur
- Young, but disciplined

The site should feel like:

> A well-designed local business consultancy with strong software capability.

Not like:

> A flashy SaaS startup.

---

## 11. Visual Design System

### 11.1 Base Palette

Use:

- Warm white / off-white for most light sections
- Friendly near-black for text and dark sections
- One KratOS brand accent
- Very light neutral gray for separators and grouped surfaces

The accent should be used sparingly.

Use it for:

- Primary CTAs
- Active states
- Small highlights
- Key numbers
- Process markers
- Important interaction states

Avoid using the accent as large decorative backgrounds unless justified.

### 11.2 Logo

Use the provided KratOS logo unchanged as the primary brand mark.

The website typography should support the logo rather than compete with it.

### 11.3 Typography

Recommended:

**English**
- Manrope
- Instrument Sans

**Sinhala**
- Noto Sans Sinhala

**Tamil**
- Noto Sans Tamil

### Typography Rules

- Use a clean humanist or grotesk sans for primary UI text
- Avoid monospace for body copy
- Use monospace only for subtle system labels, section numbers or technical details if needed
- Maintain comfortable line lengths
- Keep hierarchy obvious
- Avoid over-tight tracking
- Ensure Sinhala and Tamil layouts receive equal spacing care

---

## 12. Spacing and Rhythm

Breathing room is a core design requirement.

### Use

- Large vertical section spacing
- Short readable text blocks
- Generous margins
- Clear hierarchy
- One main idea per section
- Large screenshots
- Calm composition

### Avoid

- Dense bento layouts
- Multiple cards fighting for attention
- Stacked decorative widgets
- Tight padding
- Excessive badges
- Dashboard-style clutter

---

## 13. Component Style

### Cards

Use cards only when they genuinely improve grouping.

Preferred style:

- Flat
- Minimal borders
- Subtle neutral surface
- Moderate corner radius
- Little or no shadow

Avoid:

- Heavy shadows
- Glassmorphism
- Floating translucent panels
- Over-rounded pill cards
- Gradient borders

### Buttons

Primary:

**WhatsApp Us**

Secondary:

**Request a Quote**

Buttons should feel clear and substantial, not playful.

Avoid excessive pill styling.

### Navigation

Recommended desktop structure:

**Logo | Home | Solutions | Case Studies | How We Work | About | Contact | EN / සිං / தமிழ் | WhatsApp Us**

Mobile should prioritize:

- Menu
- Language switcher
- WhatsApp action

---

## 14. Imagery Strategy

### Preferred Imagery

- Restaurant operations
- Stock shelves

- Gradient blobs

- Generic stock-office photography
- Unnecessary device mockup overload

## 15. Homepage Strategy

### Section 1 — Hero

Goal: explain KratOS in plain language immediately.

Recommended message direction:

> **Run your business with less paperwork and more control.**

Supporting copy:

> We build software around the way your business already works.

Primary CTA:

**Message us on WhatsApp**

Secondary CTA:

**Request a Quote**

Hero visual should be:

- One strong real business image, or
- One real system screenshot, or
- A restrained split between business context and product UI

Avoid decorative complexity.

---

### Section 2 — Familiar Problems

Present problems business owners recognize.

Examples:

- Stock getting hard to track?
- Sales recorded manually?
- Reports taking too long?
- Too many books and spreadsheets?
- Hard to know what is happening across the business?

Keep copy short and practical.

---

### Section 3 — What KratOS Does

Explain custom software simply.

Core message:

> **One system, built around the way your business works.**

Explain that clients can digitize only what they need.

Potential areas:

- Sales
- Inventory
- Purchasing
- Suppliers
- Customers
- Staff
- Reports
- Billing

Do not imply every project must include all modules.

---

### Section 4 — Real Projects

This section should appear relatively early because real work is KratOS's strongest trust asset.

#### Project 01 — Restaurant

Show:

- Client logo
- Client name
- Short problem statement
- What KratOS built
- Screenshots
- Short explanation

#### Project 02 — Poultry Wholesale / Retail

Show:

- Client logo
- Client name
- Short problem statement
- What KratOS built
- Screenshots
- Short explanation

Keep case studies visual and outcome-oriented.

---

### Section 5 — What Can Be Digitized

Present common business operations without turning them into rigid packages.

Possible categories:

- Sales
- Stock
- Purchasing
- Customers
- Suppliers
- Staff
- Reporting
- Billing

Suggested copy:

> Need only a few of these? That is completely fine. We build according to your business, not a fixed package.

---

### Section 6 — How We Work

Use a simple process.

1. Message us
2. We understand your business
3. We visit / discuss your workflow
4. We define what you need
5. We send a quotation
6. We build, test and support

The visual style should feel structured and reassuring.

---

### Section 7 — Reliability and Support

Goal: answer the question:

> “Will these guys still be there after the system is delivered?”

Communicate:

- Support after deployment
- Clear communication
- Testing before launch
- Practical training / handover
- Ongoing improvements when required

Do not make promises that KratOS cannot guarantee.

---

### Section 8 — Final CTA

Keep it simple and confident.

Suggested direction:

> **Tell us how your business works. We’ll help you figure out what to digitize.**

Primary:

**Message us on WhatsApp**

Secondary:

**Request a Quote**

---

## 16. Case Study Design

Each case study should follow the same structure.

### Structure

1. Client logo + name
2. Business type
3. The problem
4. How the business worked before
5. What KratOS built
6. Key features
7. Real screenshots
8. How the workflow improved
9. CTA

Avoid exaggerated claims.

If measurable outcomes are not available, describe operational improvements honestly.

---

## 17. Copywriting Style

### Voice

- Clear
- Friendly
- Practical
- Respectful
- Confident
- Non-technical

### Avoid

- Buzzwords
- Corporate filler
- Over-promising
- Excessively technical language
- Startup clichés

### Avoid phrases such as

- Cutting-edge solutions
- Digital transformation ecosystem
- Revolutionary technology
- Seamless innovation
- Empowering businesses through next-generation solutions

### Prefer phrases such as

- Keep your stock in one place
- See your sales clearly
- Reduce manual work
- Build only what you need
- Understand your business better
- Keep your records organized
- Make daily work easier

---

## 18. Interaction and Motion

Motion should be restrained.

Recommended:

- Gentle section reveals
- Small hover movement
- Smooth image transitions
- Simple carousel transitions for projects
- Clear loading and button feedback

Avoid:

- Floating decorative objects
- Excessive parallax
- Dramatic 3D animation
- Scroll hijacking
- Constant motion

The website should feel calm and dependable.

---

## 19. Accessibility and Usability

The target audience may include users with limited technical familiarity.

Prioritize:

- Large clickable targets
- Clear navigation
- Strong contrast
- Readable body size
- Simple forms
- Obvious CTAs
- Plain language
- Responsive layouts
- Mobile-first WhatsApp access
- Clear validation messages
- Consistent behavior across all three languages

---

## 20. Design Principles

Every page and component should pass these checks:

### Friendly
Can a non-technical business owner understand what this means?

### Capable
Does this look professionally built and trustworthy?

### Relevant
Does this speak to actual local business problems?

### Simple
Can anything unnecessary be removed?

### Honest
Are we showing real proof rather than making unsupported claims?

### Local
Does the experience feel natural for Sri Lankan businesses?

### Consistent
Would the same level of care be visible in English, Sinhala and Tamil?

---

## 21. Things Explicitly Out of Scope

Do not use as the core visual language:

- Glassmorphism
- Heavy gradients
- Purple AI-style palettes
- Floating 3D objects
- Excessive bento-grid density
- Overlapping card stacks
- Startup dashboard clichés
- Cyberpunk interfaces
- Overly corporate enterprise visuals
- Excessive technical jargon
- Generic Western stock photography

---

## 22. Final Design Statement

KratOS should feel like a software company that understands local businesses before it talks about technology.

The website should reassure the client that:

- Their business does not need to change to fit the software.
- The software will be shaped around their actual workflow.
- They do not need to buy features they do not need.
- KratOS will explain the process clearly.
- KratOS has already delivered real systems.
- KratOS will support them after deployment.

The visual experience should therefore be:

**Open. Calm. Local. Clear. Professional. Reliable.**

And the core brand promise should remain:

> **Software built around the way your business works.**
