## Design decisions

- Explain the offer immediately: custom software for Sri Lankan businesses, with concrete examples of billing, stock and records.
- Keep only the orange angular mark. Remove the hero grid, rings, connectors, shadows, ornamental labels, oversized arrows and generated project covers.
- Use a readable, restrained layout: dark text, warm white, consistent alignment, limited orange and normal sentence-case headings. Give real content more room than decoration.
- Put services and the working process before examples. Describe the outcome of each delivery step so a prospective client knows what to expect.
- Treat the unverified restaurant and poultry material as illustrative business workflows, not delivered client results. Keep existing URLs so approved case studies can replace them later.
- Keep quotation and contact actions predictable. If WhatsApp is not configured, label the fallback as a conversation, not as a WhatsApp link.
- Preserve English, Sinhala and Tamil coverage. Revised translations remain drafts for the owner's review.

These choices apply recognition, familiar language, clear feedback and reduced irrelevant information from [Nielsen Norman Group's usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) and scannable headings from [How users read on the web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/). They are design judgments, not a claim of measured conversion improvements. A useful review is whether a business owner can identify the service, recognize their own workflow and explain the next step after a brief visit.
